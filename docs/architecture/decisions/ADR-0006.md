# ADR-0006: Coptic Orthodox Biblical Canon, Textual Provenance, and Content Governance Framework

**Status:** APPROVED (Phase 1.3)  
**Date:** 2026-11-17  
**Deciders:** Principal Software Architect, Theological Architecture Lead, Database Agent

---

## 1. Context and Problem Statement

The platform must accurately represent the Holy Bible according to the authentic tradition of the Coptic Orthodox Church of Alexandria.

The previous Phase 1.2 survey conflated manuscript witnesses (such as 3 Maccabees) and liturgical appendices (such as Psalm 151 and Prayer of Manasseh) with the official 73-book canon, and assumed unverified Holy Synod approvals for certain modern printed editions. Furthermore, modern digital copies of sacred texts carry copyright and typographical protections.

A technical Architecture Decision Record must **not** silently approve an unresolved theological or editorial decision. The platform must establish a clear boundary between:

1. Canonical Scripture.
2. Deuterocanonical Books.
3. Liturgical Lectionaries (Katameros).
4. Liturgical Appendices (e.g. Psalm 151, Prayer of Manasseh).
5. Historical Manuscript Witnesses (e.g. 3 Maccabees).

---

## 2. Decision Outcome

### 2.1 The Official 73-Book Coptic Orthodox Canon

The platform formally adopts the official canonical position of the Coptic Orthodox Church:

- **Total Canonical Books:** **73 Books**.
  - **Old Testament:** 46 Books (39 Protocanonical + 7 Deuterocanonical: Tobit, Judith, Wisdom of Solomon, Sirach, Baruch, 1 Maccabees, 2 Maccabees; with additions to Esther and Daniel integrated into their respective canonical books).
  - **New Testament:** 27 Books (First codified by St. Athanasius the Apostolic in 367 AD).

### 2.2 Classification of Specific Disputed / Liturgical Items

- **Psalm 151:** Modeled as a canonical psalm within the Book of Psalms (not a standalone 74th book). Chanted during Bright Saturday.
- **Prayer of Manasseh:** Modeled as a liturgical prayer / ode in liturgical collections; not enumerated as an independent book in the 73-book canon.
- **3 Maccabees:** Classified as an ancient Alexandrian historical witness. It is **EXCLUDED** from canonical Bible data tables and marked as awaiting formal Holy Synod determination.
- **Esther & Daniel Additions:** Modeled as canonical chapters/verses within Esther and Daniel respectively.

### 2.3 Text Licensing, Provenance, and Zero-Scraping Policy

- **Protocanonical Base Text:** 1865 Smith & Van Dyck Arabic Bible (verified Public Domain).
- **Deuterocanonical Text:** Ingestion is **BLOCKED** until a verified digital licensing agreement or written ecclesiastical authorization is secured from the Bible Society of Egypt (for the 1988 Synod-supervised edition) or the Coptic Orthodox Bishopric of Youth.
- **Bohairic Coptic Text:** Sourced from public-domain critical editions (Horner, Tattam, Lagarde).
- **Prohibition on Scraping:** Web scraping of commercial or third-party Coptic apps and websites is strictly prohibited. Every digital text must possess a cryptographic checksum (`checksumSha256`) and an audit trail.

### 2.4 Flexible Schema Design

To avoid destructive database migrations if theological classifications are refined:

- Book categorization uses flexible string tags and boolean attributes (`is_deuterocanonical`, `canonical_status`) rather than rigid, unchangeable database enums.
- An `alternate_reference` column preserves Septuagint and Agpeya Psalm numbering variations without breaking standard canonical cross-references.

---

## 3. Consequences

- **Positive:**
  - Absolute theological accuracy aligning with the official teaching of the Coptic Orthodox Patriarchate.
  - No legal or copyright liability from unauthorized digital text distribution.
  - Resilient database schema that accommodates future theological clarifications without table rewrites.
- **Negative:**
  - Full Bible feature launch is gated on formal Deuterocanonical text licensing, requiring clear communication in project timelines.
