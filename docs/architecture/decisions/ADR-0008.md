# ADR-0008: Deterministic Fixed-Day Coptic Calendar Mathematics & Epoch Convention

**Status:** APPROVED  
**Date:** 2026-11-17  
**Deciders:** Principal Software Architect, Frontend Agent, Backend Agent, QA Agent

---

## 1. Context and Problem Statement

The Ana Orthodox platform requires an authoritative, deterministic calendar conversion and arithmetic engine between the Coptic Civil Calendar and the Gregorian Civil Calendar.

Prior to Phase 2B.1, calendar dates were represented by static placeholders or fragile browser-specific `Intl` locale tricks. This introduced multiple architectural risks:

1. **Timezone & DST Pollution:** Treating civil dates as instant timestamps (such as standard JavaScript `Date` instances at midnight) causes dates to shift by $\pm 1$ day depending on client timezone offsets or Daylight Saving Time transitions.
2. **Duplication Risk:** Duplicating calendar mathematics separately between NestJS backend and Next.js frontend leads to synchronization drifts.
3. **Flawed Leap-Year Assumptions:** Conflating Gregorian leap-year numbering (divisible by 4) with Coptic leap-year numbering (year mod 4 === 3) results in incorrect month lengths for Nasie (Month 13).
4. **Database Overhead:** Using database tables or persistent queries for deterministic calendar math adds unnecessary I/O overhead and fails in offline PWA environments.

---

## 2. Decision Outcome

We have implemented a standalone, zero-dependency, pure TypeScript package: **`@ana-orthodox/coptic-calendar`** under `packages/coptic-calendar/`.

### 2.1 The Epoch Convention & Rata Die System

- The engine uses the **Rata Die (R.D.)** fixed-day numbering system (where Day 1 is January 1, 1 CE Gregorian), as codified by Dershowitz and Reingold (_Calendrical Calculations_):
  - **Coptic Epoch:** 1 Thout, Year 1 AM = **R.D. 103,605**.
  - In both the Julian calendar and the proleptic Gregorian calendar, this corresponds to **August 29, 284 CE**.
- The mathematical conversion from a Coptic date $(Y, M, D)$ to its fixed R.D. number is:
  $$\text{R.D.} = 103604 + 365 \times (Y - 1) + \left\lfloor\frac{Y}{4}\right\rfloor + 30 \times (M - 1) + D$$

### 2.2 Leap-Year Rule

- In the Coptic calendar, a year is a leap year if and only if:
  $$\text{floorMod}(Y, 4) \equiv 3$$
- Examples:
  - 1743 AM is a leap year ($1743 = 4 \times 435 + 3$). Nasie has **6 days**, and the year contains **366 days**.
  - 1744 AM is a common year ($1744 = 4 \times 436 + 0$). Nasie has **5 days**, and the year contains **365 days**.

### 2.3 Calendar Structure

- Months 1–12 (Thout through Mesori) have a strictly fixed length of **30 days**.
- Month 13 (Nasie / The Epagomenal Days) has:
  - **5 days** in common years.
  - **6 days** in leap years.
- Rejection of day 6 of Nasie in common years is enforced during validation with an explicit diagnostic error.

### 2.4 Date-Only Semantics & Timezone Independence

- Internal operations operate strictly on structured integer tuples: `{ year: number, month: number, day: number }`.
- When external ISO strings (`YYYY-MM-DD`) or JavaScript `Date` instances are passed, they are normalized using UTC component extraction (`getUTCFullYear()`, `getUTCMonth() + 1`, `getUTCDate()`), eliminating any host-local timezone or DST bias.

### 2.5 REST API Design

The NestJS backend exposes two lightweight, stateless endpoints under `CalendarController` (`/api/v1/calendar/coptic`):

- `GET /api/v1/calendar/coptic/from-gregorian?date=YYYY-MM-DD`
- `GET /api/v1/calendar/coptic/to-gregorian?year=Y&month=M&day=D`
  Both endpoints validate inputs using class-validator pipes and wrap results in standard `ApiSuccessResponse` envelopes.

---

## 3. Consequences

### Positive

- **100% Deterministic & Bidirectional:** Tested across complete 365-day and 366-day cycles with exact round-trip invariance ($C \to G \to C$ and $G \to C \to G$).
- **Offline & PWA Ready:** Pure calculation with zero network or database dependencies allows Next.js web clients to run calendar conversions entirely client-side when offline.
- **Zero Database Overhead:** Zero migrations or PostgreSQL queries required.
- **Ecclesiastical Accuracy:** Verified against historical benchmarks (e.g. 1 Thout 1743 AM = 11 September 2026; 29 Koiak 1743 AM = 7 January 2027 Nativity; 6 Nasie 1743 AM = 11 September 2027).

### Negative / Trade-offs

- Julian calendar historic dates prior to 1582 CE require proleptic Gregorian date mapping when interacting with standard civil ISO representations. This is explicitly documented in the API contracts.
